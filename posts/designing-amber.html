Why Amber.

A small note on why we started building a music player, of all things, and what we want it to feel like.

August 2026 Amber 4 min read

We didn't set out to build a music player. We set out to fix the fifteen seconds before the music starts.

That's the part that bothered us — not the sound, but everything around it. Opening an app and being asked to log in again. A homepage full of things we didn't ask for. A queue that quietly rearranges itself. By the time a song actually starts playing, the moment that made you want to listen has usually passed.

Amber is our attempt at getting out of that way.

Your library, not a feed.

Amber is built around a library you already own or already curate — local files, and eventually a small set of services — rather than an algorithmic front page. There's no autoplay queue trying to keep you scrolling. Opening Amber shows you what you left open last, nothing more.

That sounds like a small decision. In practice it changes almost everything about the interface: no recommendation rail, no "for you" carousel, no notification badge asking for attention. Just the library, and whatever you're playing.

Built for the background.

Most of the time, a music player isn't the app you're looking at — it's the app running quietly behind whatever you're actually doing. So a lot of Amber's design effort has gone into things you'll mostly interact with from outside the app itself: the menu bar controls, Now Playing integration, keyboard shortcuts that don't fight with anything else running.

"The best compliment for Amber would be forgetting it's there."

We spent an embarrassing number of days on the menu bar icon alone — how it animates when a track changes, how it behaves with dozens of other icons crowded next to it, how it looks in both light and dark menu bars without ever feeling loud.

What we're still deciding.

Lyrics, equalizer presets, smart playlists — the usual list of features a music player is expected to have. We're being slow about adding them. Each one is a chance to reintroduce the clutter we started this project to remove, so they only go in if they can do that without adding noise.

Amber is still early. The player works, the library works, and the parts that don't feel quiet yet haven't shipped. That's on purpose — we'd rather take longer and keep it small than rush toward a feature list.
