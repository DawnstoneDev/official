Amber is now available.

A quiet music player from Dawnstone, now available on the web.

August 2026 Amber 3 min read

We built Amber around a simple idea: listening should feel quiet.

Today, Amber is available on the web. It is the first public web edition of Dawnstone's music player — a small experiment in building a music experience that feels calm, focused, and deliberately uncomplicated.

Amber is made for the music you already have. Instead of asking you to move your library somewhere else, it lets you bring local music directly into the player and begin listening.

Music without the noise.

Music players can become complicated very quickly. Libraries become crowded. Controls multiply. Features compete for attention. Amber takes a different approach.

The interface stays intentionally restrained. Dark surfaces. Warm amber light. Soft translucency. Subtle movement. Everything is there for a reason.

“The best music player is the one that lets you forget you're using one.”

Built around listening.

Amber understands the details inside your music. When you add a track, its metadata can be read automatically, including its title, artist, album, and artwork.

Artists have their own place in the library. Albums are grouped naturally. Songs stay easy to browse. And when you select an artist, Amber can take you directly to their music.

The result is a library that feels less like a database and more like a collection.

Make a playlist. Keep it yours.

Playlists are a central part of Amber. Create one from your library, add tracks to it, reorder songs, remove tracks, and play the entire collection whenever you want.

There is no account to manage. No social layer. No recommendation engine trying to decide what you should hear next.

Just your music, organized the way you want it.

More control, when you want it.

Amber also includes a playback queue, shuffle and repeat modes, a nine-band equalizer, and a lightweight visualizer.

They are there when you need them, and stay out of the way when you don't.

The goal is not to turn Amber into a wall of controls. It is to give you enough control to make the experience feel like your own.

Designed for the Mac.

Amber began as a native macOS project. That origin still shapes the way we think about the application.

Clear hierarchy. Familiar controls. Generous spacing. Subtle translucency. Warm light against a quiet background.

The web edition brings that same design language to the browser while keeping the experience simple enough to open and use immediately.

This is only the beginning.

Amber is still evolving. There are ideas we have not built yet, interactions we want to refine, and details we are still learning from.

That is part of what makes this release meaningful to us. It is not the final version of Amber. It is the first time we can put it in front of people and say: this is what we've been building.

We hope it feels good to use.
